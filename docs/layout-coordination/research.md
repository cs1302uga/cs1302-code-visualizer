# Separate Placement, Repair, and Routing Deliberately

**Independent stack and heap placement models should feed a shared coordinator that owns bounded geometric repair; connector routing should consume a coherent measured scene and return a validated plan or structured failure.** Preserve existing placement and repair policies during that extraction. Literature supports separating geometry, search, and drawing, but cross-region references still couple their quality. Exact graph search supplies a restricted comparison oracle, while sparse constructions, temporal anchors, dynamic programming, and memoization require distinct assumptions and measurements. None establishes a universal speedup, an optimal drawing sequence, or improved Java comprehension. This synthesis recommends staged experiments under the original **≤300 ms fresh-layout protocol**, without authorizing global rearrangement, default enablement, release, or approval.

## Graph size, search cost, and painted validity differ

Published orthogonal visibility routing supplies a precise geometric baseline. Wybrow et al. (2010) intersect horizontal and vertical visibility segments from rectangular obstacles and connector points, obtaining **O(n²) vertices, edges, and construction time**, with stated single-connector search O(n² log n). Route preservation concerns that obstacle/port model and length/bends; ordering and nudging follow search. Marriott et al. (2014) instead use directed 1-bend visibility: **O(n) vertices, O(n²) edges, and O(n²) construction**, assuming fixed ports per object. Their three C++ benchmark diagrams show lower routing times alongside higher construction costs and larger edge sets. This application's 10-pixel port enumeration violates any automatic constant-port assumption. ([Orthogonal routing manuscript](https://users.monash.edu/~mwybrow/papers/wybrow-gd-2009.pdf); [1-bend manuscript](https://users.monash.edu/~mwybrow/papers/marriott-diagrams-2014.pdf))

The 2014 canonicality claim requires qualification: the accessible manuscript states/proves obstacle-hugging canonicality results, whereas the publisher abstract calls canonicality a conjecture; the full published chapter was inaccessible. Neither establishes universal canonicality under this application's shaft crossings, source exceptions, variable ports, or sequential occupancy. Separately, a Cartesian ruler grid has up to `X·Y` positions and constant-degree adjacency: it is sparse relative to its own vertices, yet potentially quadratic in input geometry. Lazy generation saves allocation without removing that search space. ([Publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-44043-8_4); [routing analysis](research-notes/orthogonal-routing.md))

Practical sparsification should preserve choices before reducing counts. Visibility sweeps can avoid irrelevant crossings; collinear degree-two contraction is safe only without ports, turns, clearance changes, or other state boundaries. Corner-only straight edges fail even for diagonal endpoints in empty space, which require an elbow or equivalent macro-edge. A 1-bend macro-edge must retain both elbow alternatives when applicable, internal bend cost, departure/arrival orientation, and segment geometry for validation. These are application deductions, not measured optimizations. Graph-build time, geometric queries, memory, and final rejection rates must accompany vertex counts.

Direction-aware state is essential for bend costs. Horizontal/vertical orientation suffices for axis-change penalties; signed directions are needed when reversals or port normals affect future choices. Proposed complete-route objectives should charge source and terminal arcs explicitly, but an oracle reproducing current search must reproduce its endpoint omissions. Dijkstra on nonnegative costs or admissible A* with correct reopening/termination is exact **only on the same graph, states, ports, constraints, and cost** (Dijkstra, 1959; Hart et al., 1968). Minimum Manhattan distance over all admissible goals is a safe basic lower bound; stronger bend/terminal bounds require a proved relaxation and minimization over complete per-port bounds, rather than adding bends only to the nearest port. ([Dijkstra original](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/JavaAndDS/shortestPath/1959dijkstra.pdf); [A* original](https://ai.stanford.edu/~nilsson/OnlinePubs-Nils/PublishedPapers/astar.pdf))

Weighted A* sacrifices guaranteed optimality; under appropriate assumptions it can bound graph cost by a weight times optimum (Likhachev et al., 2003). Caps can prevent discovery, although a correctly popped goal before exhaustion can retain an otherwise applicable weighted bound. An arbitrary interrupted incumbent needs a valid frontier-bound certificate. Rounding, rejection, changed margins, body relaxation, and repair do not automatically inherit that bound. Painted validation must remain authoritative: unconditional shaft rectangles would forbid permitted transverse crossings, while centerlines omit stroke, head, curve, and source rules. Exact search on restricted geometry also proves neither continuous-space completeness nor globally best sequential routing. ([ARA* paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~maxim/files/ara_nips03.pdf); [painted-clearance implementation](https://github.com/cs1302uga/cs1302-code-visualizer/blob/b6b2e2b9a0593662ac336016706809151cfa1c04/cs1302_code_visualizer/frontend/js/shaftClearance.ts#L1-L109))

## Temporal anchors need explicit identities and objectives

Stability has task-specific support. An orientation experiment with 28 participants found faster, more accurate node-location and path-following under mental-map preservation; the authors' review abstract describes inconclusive earlier comprehension evidence (Archambault & Purchase, 2013a, 2013b). These findings concern different tasks, and the inaccessible full review cannot refute the experiment. Neither tests Java frame insertion. Under fixed dimensions and a top origin, inserting above translates all surviving frames; appending below avoids that movement but can hide the active frame. Width changes, camera fitting, and new obstacles complicate both. Alternative ordering remains a separately evaluated policy. ([Review abstract](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/the-map-in-the-mental-map-experimental-results-in-dynamic-graph-d/); [orientation study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232747172_Mental_Map_Preservation_Helps_User_Orientation_in_Dynamic_Graphs))

Full-trace planning should index displayed membership, changing dimensions, ports, references, and visibility gaps. Captured presence is not displayed presence; first/last appearance in sampled data is not allocation/deallocation evidence. Heap identities require a nonreuse guarantee or generation discriminator. Modern frame keys use method, line, and array index, so they can change within an invocation or recur across invocations; they are not guaranteed call identities. Selected snapshot payloads can omit history entirely. Fonts, viewport, filtering, string mode, trace revision/horizon, and strategy version therefore belong in the planning context. ([Identity and payload audit](research-notes/algorithm-audit.md))

**There is no objective-independent peak heap.** Object count, width sum, height sum, and area sum can peak at different times with disjoint sets. Width/height sums describe single-row/column demand; box area only lower-bounds packing area and excludes channels. Maximum actual drawing area differs from the product of independently maximal width and height. Retain tied peaks or document selection. Chronologically adjacent peak intersections can preserve bridges lost by intersecting all peaks, but endpoint intersection does not prove persistence: compute membership across every intervening state or justify contiguous display intervals. Even continuous membership proves neither unchanged sizes nor reusable routes. Peak anchors are proposed candidate-selection heuristics, not published optimality results. ([Temporal derivations](research-notes/temporal-placement.md))

Offline coupling differs from sequential stability. Brandes and Mader's linking approach couples layouts across time and performs well in their stress/movement experiments; their faster anchoring alternative and competing quality measures limit transfer (2012). Dynamic-tree research shows how noncoexisting nodes can share space, but its linear-time theorem assumes an acyclic supergraph and specified tree criteria (Skambath & Tantau, 2016). These results motivate foresight without proving optimal box packing, peak anchors, or reusable routes here. ([Offline comparison](https://kim246.wwwdns.kim.uni-konstanz.de/publications/bm-qcsma-12.pdf); [dynamic-tree manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1608.08385v1))

## Dynamic programming and memoization solve different obligations

A bounded candidate DP is defensible when its state retains everything affecting future cost or feasibility. For feasible candidates `C_t`, within-step cost `q_t`, and transition cost `m_t`, use `D_t(s) = q_t(s) + min_r[D_(t−1)(r) + m_t(r,s)]`, with explicit start/terminal costs and backpointers. State may require routes, reservations, last-seen positions, or budgets alongside both placements. Higher-order smoothness requires history augmentation (Bertsekas, 1987). With at most K candidates per step, this needs O(TK²) transition evaluations and O(TK) backpointers, excluding generation and geometry evaluation. Exactness means minimum over retained candidates and transitions, not unrestricted coordinates. ([DP foundation](https://web.mit.edu/dimitrib/www/Bertsekas1987DP.pdf))

Locally optimal states do not imply an optimal sequence: if A costs 0 and B costs 1 initially, only B is feasible next, and A→B costs 10 while B→B costs 0, local choices cost 10 versus 1. Fixed-anchor forward/backward DP is exact only conditional on sufficient boundary states and retained candidates; choosing peak layouts first can discard the best sequence. Combining forward and backward scores requires the same complete state and no double counting. Routing on cyclic graphs likewise needs shortest-path search, not naive recursive memoization; a one-pass DP requires a proved DAG restriction, which can exclude necessary backtracking. ([Application recurrence and counterexample](research-notes/temporal-placement.md))

Memoization reuses equivalent subproblems; caching greedy choices adds no optimization proof. Canonical settled output should be a deterministic function of trace/context and target step, identical for forward, backward, direct, and random seeks. Whole-trace planning or chronological replay from sufficient checkpoints can satisfy this without temporal optimality. Geometry caches need obstacle revisions, occupied shafts, ports, identities, widths, policies, and repair context; endpoint equality alone is insufficient. The `971e893` comparison already includes heuristic memoization and geometry indexes, so proposals must identify remaining work. LPA* supports repair across related searches, but requires stable graph identities and correct updates; it promises no cheaper cold initialization (Koenig et al., 2004). ([Comparison search](https://github.com/cs1302uga/cs1302-code-visualizer/blob/971e89341e9fba85459ff3ad1d1dc7aac4145e5b/cs1302_code_visualizer/frontend/js/referenceRouting.ts#L206-L268); [LPA* paper and erratum](https://idm-lab.org/bib/abstracts/Koen04a.html))

A further application hypothesis is reverse multi-source Dijkstra for many queries sharing a fixed graph, goals, direction state, and terminal costs. Its table can amortize exact remaining costs. Distances on a valid relaxed graph remain lower bounds when occupancy only removes edges or raises costs; obstacle removal or cost reduction can invalidate old bounds. Source/target exceptions must also respect the relaxation. Count preprocessing and memory, and compare against existing port-distance memoization. Likewise, balanced rectangle indexes do not guarantee logarithmic queries when overlapping bounds force broad traversal. Neither technique warrants a universal speedup claim. ([Routing deductions](research-notes/orthogonal-routing.md))

## Measure staged hypotheses under the original protocol

First extract coordinator ownership while retaining behavior. Then instrument ruler/state counts, stale removals, port evaluations, geometry queries, repair attempts, construction/search/validation time, and memory. ## Conclusion

The practical opportunity is to make expensive decisions replaceable and their guarantees inspectable. Revisioned geometry, bounded repair ownership, sufficient temporal state, and deterministic reconstruction let future experiments change one assumption at a time. A faster stage or smaller graph becomes useful evidence only when the complete visualizer preserves semantics and passes the unchanged rendered protocol.
