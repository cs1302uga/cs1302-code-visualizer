## What graph and search model can preserve route quality?

### Takeaway

Treat graph construction, graph-search correctness, and final painted-route validity as separate obligations. A Cartesian ruler grid can have constant-degree adjacency and still contain quadratically many geometric intersections; a graph with fewer vertices can have more edges and higher construction cost. Preserve incoming orientation when charging bends. Use Dijkstra or admissible A\* as an exact oracle only on the SAME graph and cost, and measure weighted or compressed alternatives against that limited oracle.

### Cited Findings

- **C1 — Orthogonal visibility construction; high confidence within the paper's model.** Wybrow et al. (2010; GD 2009) construct horizontal/vertical visible segments from rectangle corners and connector points, then intersect them. Their graph has `O(n²)` vertices and edges and `O(n²)` construction time; the sweep substeps take `O(n log n)`. Their route-preservation observation maps a valid route to this graph without increasing length or bends. Search retains entry direction and uses Manhattan distance plus a bend lower bound; the stated single-connector search bound is `O(n² log n)`. A separate stage orders/nudges shared segments. These are rectangular-obstacle, fixed-port, length/bend results, not this application's full clearance or sharing contract. Evidence: manuscript §§3–5, observation, Theorems 1–2. [Author manuscript](https://users.monash.edu/~mwybrow/papers/wybrow-gd-2009.pdf); [publisher and bibliographic record](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-11805-0_22).
- **C2 — Fewer vertices does not imply fewer edges; high confidence for reported sizes/measurements, qualified for canonicality.** Marriott et al. (2014) replace intersection vertices with a directed 1-bend visibility construction over corners/endpoints and direction. It has `O(n)` vertices, `O(n²)` edges, and `O(n²)` construction time, assuming a fixed number of ports per object. The author manuscript gives existence/canonicality theorems for obstacle-hugging routes. Its three C++ benchmark diagrams show larger edge sets and higher graph-construction times but lower routing times than the prior graph. Thus its reported routing gains are not a guarantee of lower total cost here. **Version discrepancy:** the publisher abstract calls canonicality a conjecture; the accessible author manuscript states/proves it. Do not present canonicality as an independently verified universal theorem. Evidence: manuscript §§2–4, Theorems 1–3, Table 1. [Author manuscript](https://users.monash.edu/~mwybrow/papers/marriott-diagrams-2014.pdf); [publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-44043-8_4).
- **C3 — Exact graph-search foundation; high confidence.** Dijkstra's original second problem constructs shortest paths in increasing path-length order, relying on the shortest-prefix property; it also allows direction-dependent branch lengths. Hart et al. (1968), Theorem 1, establish A\* admissibility when its remaining-cost estimate is a lower bound. Their historical use of “admissible algorithm” denotes finding an optimum; it should not be confused with a claim that A\* is always fastest in elapsed time. [Dijkstra original paper, university-hosted scan](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/JavaAndDS/shortestPath/1959dijkstra.pdf); [Hart et al., author-hosted original](https://ai.stanford.edu/~nilsson/OnlinePubs-Nils/PublishedPapers/astar.pdf).
- **C4 — Weighted and anytime bounds; high confidence under stated assumptions.** Likhachev et al. (2003), §2.1, distinguish ordinary A\* from `g + εh`: inflation can destroy admissibility; their weighted search gives an `ε` solution-cost bound under its assumptions. ARA\* tightens bounds while reusing work across iterations. This is a quality/time tradeoff, not a wall-clock guarantee or evidence that any particular weight is faster in this renderer. Evidence: §§2.1–2.2 and their termination/update rules. [Author paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~maxim/files/ara_nips03.pdf); [original proceedings record](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2003/hash/ee8fe9093fbbb687bef15a38facc44d2-Abstract.html).

### Inferences

The following are original application-oriented deductions. They are not new performance results or claims that the cited papers prove this renderer correct.

**Ruler grids and actual sparsity.** Let `kx` and `ky` be distinct ruler counts after deduplication. The implicit graph contains up to `V = kx * ky` positions and at most four neighbors per position. Its edge count is `O(V)`, so it is sparse relative to its OWN vertex count, but `V = Θ(k²)` when both ruler counts scale with obstacle/port/channel count `k`. Calling it “sparse” without specifying the parameter hides that quadratic expansion. Lazy node/edge generation avoids eagerly allocating every intersection; it does not eliminate the potential search space. Collinear duplicates and nearly identical float coordinates affect `kx`/`ky`, so collect these counts before choosing a graph replacement.

**Construction options worth distinguishing.**

1. Clip visible horizontal/vertical rays at their first blocking obstacles, using event sweeps and interval structures. Materialize only ray intersections that represent useful turns or connections, or generate them lazily. This removes irrelevant Cartesian crossings in blocked areas but has no general linear-size guarantee; the published orthogonal construction remains quadratic (C1).
2. Contract a chain of collinear degree-two vertices into one edge only when the removed vertices are not ports, branch/turn choices, clearance-change points, or other state boundaries. Sum edge lengths and retain traversal direction. This preserves the SAME graph's path costs when the stated conditions hold; it does not prove continuous-space optimality. A raw geometric intersection with a possible perpendicular turn cannot simply be deleted.
3. Evaluate the published directed 1-bend construction (C2), preserving both possible elbow geometries and their departure/arrival directions where applicable. A macro-edge must contain its bend cost and enough geometry for clearance validation; its cost is not merely endpoint distance. Do not replace this construction with arbitrary corner-to-corner links and call the result equivalent.
4. A corner-only graph with only straight axis-aligned edges can fail in an empty scene: endpoints `(0,0)` and `(10,10)` need a Steiner elbow at `(0,10)` or `(10,0)`, or an equivalent 1-bend macro-edge. This is a counterexample to pruning every intersection that is not an obstacle corner.

**Expanded state and additive costs.** For the present scalar objective, write a state as `(position, incoming orientation)` and an edge cost as `L1(segment) + B * turn`, with `B = 18` only when reproducing the existing internal objective. A coordinate-only distance table is unsound: arrivals costing 10 horizontally and 11 vertically, followed by a vertical edge of length 10, give totals 38 and 21 respectively. Keeping only the cheaper coordinate label loses the optimum.

Horizontal/vertical is enough for a cost that distinguishes only 90° axis changes and has no direction-specific future constraints. Use four cardinal directions when reversals, source/target normals, or directional port restrictions matter. A reversal has the same axis but a different direction. A “none” state at a pooled departure intentionally omits the turn from the source stub; that is a different objective from complete-route bend count. General non-additive functions of total length and bends may require multiple nondominated labels per state; the single-label reasoning here is for an explicitly additive scalar cost.

**Multiple starts, goals, and ports.** Represent permitted departures with a super-source whose arcs charge their actual stub costs and establish incoming direction. Represent arrivals with terminal arcs charging required final geometry and turns. Remove inadmissible ports before search or encode their restrictions; arbitrary free goals can underprice an endpoint. The current terminal stub lengths are constructed uniformly, but the terminal turn still depends on entry orientation. An oracle reproducing current behavior must reproduce its omissions; a proposed fuller objective must be a separately named experiment.

A safe basic estimate is `h(v,d) = min_q L1(v,q)` over ALL admissible arrival points. For axis edges whose cost includes length plus nonnegative penalties, this estimate is consistent by the triangle inequality. A stronger candidate is `min_q [L1(v,q) + B*bmin(v,d,q,normal_q) + terminalLowerBound(q)]`, where `bmin` is proved for a relaxed obstacle-free problem. Computing exact distances in a valid relaxation provides a route to consistency; an improvised bend formula needs proof or reopen-capable admissible search.

Do not choose the nearest port and then attach that port's bend penalty. Example: current direction horizontal at `(0,0)`, ports `(0,10)` and `(12,0)`, unrestricted final direction, bend cost 18. The nearer port has relaxed cost at least 28; the farther straight port costs 12. The nearer-port-only estimate overestimates the cost to ANY goal. Take the minimum of the complete per-port bound. Conversely, scanning every port for every generated state can itself be expensive; measure that overhead.

**Exact versus bounded search.** On a finite fixed expanded graph with nonnegative arc costs, Dijkstra (`h = 0`) is a reference method. Admissible A\* with correct reopening/termination is exact; a consistent heuristic permits settling each state once. The proposed comparison must keep the graph, admissible ports, obstacle policy, orientation state, endpoint costs, tie policy where relevant, and cost function identical. Changing the graph while also changing the heuristic prevents attributing a quality gap to search alone.

For weighted A\*, state precisely what is bounded: under the appropriate lower-bound/reopening/termination assumptions, the returned graph cost can be bounded by `w * C*`. A queue-removal limit removes completeness if it aborts before a solution. It does not, by itself, invalidate the usual bound for a goal correctly popped before that limit. An arbitrary incumbent returned on interruption needs its own lower-bound certificate. A proposed certified alternative can retain an incumbent upper bound `C` and a valid frontier lower bound `L`, terminating when `C <= w*L`; proving that frontier invariant is part of the algorithm, not a consequence of having a heap. Later rejection, rounding, port fallback, obstacle relaxation, or rerunning on a different margin graph does not automatically inherit a single factor for the complete router. A cost factor also does not bound bends, visual ambiguity, crossings, or each sharing member independently.

**Resolution and clearance are independent from search exactness.** A uniform pixel grid can omit a legal fractional-width passage. Event coordinates derived from obstacle/port geometry avoid a fixed sampling resolution, but completeness still depends on which events and clearance boundaries are included. Finite search margins and queue caps are additional restrictions. An exact solve of a restricted graph can therefore return no path even when some continuous route exists. Compare a changed graph to its own exact oracle, then separately assess the graph model against exhaustive small geometric cases; never label a fine-grid approximation an exact continuous-space oracle.

### Gaps

- No graph replacement or lower-bound formula has been implemented or measured here. Relative construction, search, allocation, and final-validation costs remain unknown for current scenes.
- The full published 2014 chapter was not accessible; the author manuscript and publisher abstract disagree on canonicality's status. Generalization to overlapping inflated obstacles, degenerate geometry, many variable ports, allowed shaft crossings, and source exceptions needs a separate proof or conservative validation.
- Historical “A\* optimal efficiency” debates and Pohl's priority for weighted search were not relied upon. These notes use Hart's minimum-cost result and the original ARA\* paper's concrete weighted-search formulation, not a claim of universal minimum expansions or historical priority.

### Inferences

**Model the legal geometry before optimizing the graph.** Inflating every occupied shaft envelope into an unconditional obstacle would eliminate permitted perpendicular crossings and historical alias behavior. For orthogonal macro-edges, test each constituent horizontal/vertical segment with the actual direction-sensitive shaft predicate. For rounded corners, keep the current conservative validation after reconstruction. A broad-phase rectangle index may reject impossible overlaps cheaply, but surviving candidates still require the authoritative predicate. Conservative rejection preserves safety while potentially sacrificing completeness; a false clearance result is a correctness failure.

Obstacle expansion by half stroke width plus required rectangular margin is a useful centerline reduction for straight axis-aligned routes around rectangular objects. It is not a proof for arrowhead triangles, source exceptions, nearly parallel shafts, or rounded cut-ins. The six-pixel shaft gap includes both stroke extents and approximation errors. Treating a centerline path as the whole painted connector would silently drop those obligations. A shortest route can also be visually poor because of tight source departures or ambiguous shared arrivals, which are currently handled by policy and validation.

**Preserve terminal and identity state.** Directional ports and target identities are not interchangeable even when target rectangles coincide. Head selection may need a port identifier in addition to position; merging two equal-position goals with incompatible normals can lose necessary terminal choices. Complete route length includes source/arrival stubs and head geometry; complete bend count includes their joins. Any new objective must declare whether it is optimizing the raw grid path, unrounded complete polyline, or painted curve. Do not silently compare one against another.

**Graph quality versus full-router quality.** The existing algorithm intentionally returns early from ordered candidate families. A grid fallback's optimum does not prove that earlier accepted candidates, later body-crossing relaxation, scene ordering, or object repair are globally optimal. If the first returned graph path fails every final painted variant, that search has not enumerated the next feasible graph path; a post-validation failure is not a proof that no valid complete route exists. Similarly, C9 is a vector of per-member and scene constraints, not a scalar trunk-length objective. A trunk that reduces total unique ink while lengthening one member fails admission; a graph-search optimum cannot waive that rule. Keep a validated baseline and run optional sharing only through its existing admission/rollback boundary. Historical implicit alias exemptions must not be substituted for the controlled planner's explicit ownership.

**Search caching and geometry caching require different keys.** The present WeakMaps reuse route flattening/channel calculations by route identity and width. Their validity assumes the cached geometry/width has not changed underneath the key. A cross-search visibility cache additionally depends on obstacle geometry/version, occupied-route set and maximum stroke widths, target identity, strict/alias policy, margin/region, and the relevant source/target exceptions. A target-only or endpoint-only memo key is insufficient. Cache negative results as well as positives only when those dependencies match.

Numeric coordinate canonicalization can reduce duplicate hint rulers, but rounding a hint is not permission to move painted geometry or accept contact. Cache invalidation after source sizing, font readiness, compact-string mode, hover-width changes, repair, filter/reset, or reassignment must be explicit. Prefer immutable geometry snapshots or stable scene revisions. Recompute final results cold for direct-seek and restoration comparisons; route choice must remain a deterministic function of the permitted input context.

**Practical asymptotics for this implementation.** With `O` local obstacle rectangles, `S` flattened occupied shaft pieces, `P` arrival ports, and `V = kx*ky`, coordinate collection/sorting costs about `O(k log k)`. The current uncached heuristic costs `O(P)` per evaluation; `971e893` already memoizes it per position. Current first-time edge checks can scan `O + S` geometry; lazy caching limits repeated evaluation within a search, but rounding and complete candidate validation add separate work. A binary-heap exact solve is `O((V+E) log V)` plus geometric query costs, with a constant orientation factor. Weighted/reopen-capable search needs actual expansion/re-expansion counts; fewer visited positions does not imply lower runtime.

A sparse macro-graph can reduce states while adding edges, spatial-index builds, elbow geometry, and allocations. Reusing a graph across many routes is attractive only when request-specific source/target exclusions, occupied routes, and shaft policies can be overlaid safely. Even then each routed connector changes occupancy for later connectors. Measure build amortization and query distributions, especially the many-small-group case; do not infer a win from an asymptotic bound alone. The existing `971e893` indexes make “add a spatial index” an incomplete recommendation unless the proposed new scope and avoided work are identified.

### Gaps

- Static reads cannot identify the current dominant runtime stage or show that any candidate meets 300 ms. Existing historical measurements in coordinator context were not reproduced and are not a new acceptance result.
- The entire legal painted-route set is richer than the rectangle model in the papers. Completeness and optimality under source exceptions, curves, alias policy, and sequential scene occupancy remain unproved.
- A new application-specific sparse graph must specify how all legal orientation transitions and endpoint choices are represented. There is no evidence here that arbitrary nearest-neighbor pruning, a port cap, or a smaller margin preserves required routes.

### Inferences

**Where DP is valid.** For a fixed expanded graph, the shortest-path optimal-substructure recurrence is `D(v,d) = min [D(u,dprev) + edgeCost((u,dprev),(v,d))]`. Dijkstra/A\* are appropriate ways of evaluating the cyclic graph; writing recursive memoization does not remove the cycles. A one-pass DAG DP is valid only after proving a monotone acyclic subproblem, such as a fixed corridor where every transition advances a coordinate and cannot revisit a state. That restriction changes feasibility: a wall requiring initial movement away from the target defeats a target-monotone DP. Retain unrestricted fallback or state a narrower supported problem.

For many queries on one genuinely fixed graph/cost/goal set, a reverse multi-source Dijkstra table can supply exact remaining costs, including terminal arc costs and direction. This may amortize across sources. If actual occupancy only removes edges or adds nonnegative costs relative to a static relaxed graph, its reverse distances can remain admissible lower bounds; if obstacles are removed or costs decrease, old distances may overestimate and require repair/recomputation. Check source/target exception differences before declaring one graph a relaxation. Table construction and memory must count in total time.

Group assignment or optional-sharing DP is valid only if its state contains all frontier occupancy, style, port, and per-member constraints needed for future decisions. “Best route to target X” or “best first i references” alone does not: different preceding routes block different corridors, and an apparently better partial score can leave no valid continuation. General memoization over full scene state can have enormous key/state space. Prefer caches of pure geometric subcomputations over a claim that a small DP solves coordinated layout.

**Where incremental methods fit.** Separate geometry maintenance from shortest-path repair. An invisibility dependency index can track why a static segment is blocked. LPA\* can then repair path labels for a stable graph after actual edge changes; D\* Lite addresses a related moving-start setting. Neither supplies a correct graph update mechanism automatically. Sorting event rulers after one new coordinate can renumber many Cartesian IDs, invalidating naive index-based state reuse even when geometry barely changes. Stable geometric keys or a fixed event graph are prerequisites for useful reuse.

Trace navigation, font/layout changes, object resizing, and sequential occupied shafts may create widespread changes. Maintain a pure cold-recompute result as the semantic reference and compare backward/forward/direct seek, filter/reset, and style restoration. Incremental speedups matter only if cache maintenance and invalidation cost less than rebuilding, including first-use costs. Given the 300 ms fresh-layout gate, cold graph/search improvements are a more direct first hypothesis than navigation-only incremental repair.

**Plausible measured experiments, not implementation commitments.**

1. **Decompose work first.** Count `kx`, `ky`, potential/visited orientation states, queue removals, stale removals, improved-label reinsertions, first-time visibility tests, geometric candidates returned by indexes, port-bound evaluations, constructed graphs, and final validation calls. Time construction, search, candidate validation, sharing admission, measurement/layout, and historical initialization separately. Collect detailed counters in supporting runs; do not silently add instrumentation overhead to one side of the acceptance comparison.
2. **SAME-graph search matrix.** On saved small and adversarial production geometry, compare Dijkstra, ordinary A\* with minimum-port Manhattan, a proved obstacle-free direction/port relaxation, and weights 1.1/1.25/1.5. Keep the cost exactly fixed. Record cost ratio to the exact oracle, bends/length separately, expansions/re-expansions, runtime, failures, and cap exhaustion. Oracle runs can be offline and uncapped; that does not authorize removing or changing production limits. A passing weighted ratio on sampled cases is empirical evidence, not a proof for unseen graphs.
3. **Graph representation matrix.** Hold endpoints, clearance predicates, and cost policy fixed while comparing the existing lazy Cartesian graph, provably safe collinear contraction, and directed 1-bend construction. Solve EACH representation exactly for small cases before comparing weighted searches. Verify preservation against exhaustive paths on the original finite graph where equivalence is claimed. Report graph-build time, edge/vertex count, memory, post-validation rejection, and total time. A lower cost on a richer graph is a model difference, not failure of exact search on a smaller graph.

**Rejected techniques and reasons.**

- Coordinate-only Dijkstra/A\*, or memoization keyed only by position: loses bend/orientation state.
- A full Cartesian product described as `O(n)` “sparse visibility”: confuses constant degree with total geometry size. A 1-bend graph described as edge-sparse is also unsupported by its quadratic edge bound.
- Fixed coarse grids, arbitrary nearest-neighbor pruning, port truncation, or small fixed margins presented as exact: can remove a valid corridor/arrival. A bounded heuristic fallback may use such restrictions only with explicit scope/failure reporting, not an exactness claim.
- Multiplying Manhattan distance by 1.25 and calling it admissible A\*, or using the weighted implementation as the exact oracle: changes the guarantee.
- Returning the first arbitrary incumbent when a wall-clock timer expires and claiming a weighted bound: lacks the required termination/lower-bound certificate and conflicts with the original no-cutoff protocol.
- Blocking all inflated shaft boxes or accepting sampled curve clearance: changes crossing/alias semantics or weakens conservative painted checks.
- Unconditional LPA\*/D\* Lite integration for fresh layouts: no established reuse benefit; graph maintenance and stable identity still have to be solved.
- All-pairs shortest paths or unconstrained scene-state DP as the first optimization: potentially high preprocessing/memory with request-dependent occupancy and few reusable exact problems; no amortization evidence here.
- Importing any-angle sparse-visibility/taut-path results as an orthogonal bend-cost proof: their motion/cost model differs. Such search results were screened out rather than used as evidence.
- General target-wide Steiner/hyperedge routing, global rearrangement, or library replacement as an in-scope fix: these change the problem and do not preserve optional-sharing admission automatically. A smaller total trunk does not establish member quality or source-to-target semantics.
- Repeating already attempted index proposals without a new measured hypothesis: coordinator evidence records earlier grid-line/protected-paint/per-segment variants reverted for insufficient rendered benefit. That is historical context, not proof that every future variant loses. [Recorded comparison context](https://github.com/cs1302uga/cs1302-code-visualizer/pull/28).
